# AHMED — the story

Guidance for anyone — person or assistant — working on this game. **This is
the canon**, and it applies to all three builds: the browser game in this
folder, the Unreal port in `../ahmed-fighter-ue5`, and the Unity port in
`../ahmed-fighter-unity`.

Recorded 2026-09-07 (Riyadh), from the game's author.

---

## The story

**A boy called Ahmed, who loves MMA.**

He grows up on it and he is good at it. Young — younger than anyone expects —
he turns professional. That is the whole first act: a kid who fights, and
wins, and is somebody.

**Then he falls.**

He is walking. Nothing is happening. There is a hole in the street, and he
goes down it.

**He comes out in AL-HALQA.**

And in that place the only thing he has is the one thing he brought: he can
fight. So he does. He fights his way through it, and as he goes he finds
**talents**, he gains **levels**, and he buys **upgrades** — which is not a
game system bolted onto a story, it is the story. He arrived with nothing but
his hands and he is building himself back up, one fight at a time.

**And by the end he does not want to go home any more.**

That is the ending. He arrives wanting the way up and spends the whole game
looking for it. What he finds instead is that down here, for the first time,
he is somebody — and the last thing he does is walk into the arena on purpose.

---

## The world

**AL-HALQA — الحلقة — the Ring.**

It is its own place. It is not Kuwait, it is not anywhere, and it is not a
warped version of anywhere. Nobody there has heard of the world Ahmed fell out
of, and the game never shows it: we never see above, it is never named, and
the only evidence it exists is Ahmed saying so. That is deliberate. If the
player never sees home, the player cannot miss it either — and when Ahmed
stops wanting it, nothing has been taken from anyone.

*Halqa* is a ring, a circle, a link in a chain, and the ring of onlookers that
closes around a fight in a marketplace. All four meanings are the place.

**The world is a closed loop, and that is not decoration — it is the map.**
Nine areas in a circle: the souq, the striking house, the dawn harbour, the
salt towers, the neon crescent, the stone island, the dead road, the camp of
fires, and back to the souq. Every link between them is sealed until Ahmed
learns the thing that opens it.

There is exactly one way out of the circle. It is a door off the souq, it is
the only link in the world that is not a street, and it needs the one talent
that is not a physical skill. **It does not lead out. It leads to the middle,
and the middle is the arena, and the arena's only exit is back to the souq.**

Ahmed spends the game looking for the way up. What the map actually is, and
what he works out far too late, is a wheel with a room at the hub.

---

## The arc

**One.** He lands in the souq and starts asking where the hole is. Nobody
understands the question. There is a crew leaning on the traders and he stops
being a bystander, because that is what he does with a room he does not
understand. Word travels, and the striking house wants to see what he can do.

They give him **VAULT** — how to climb. He takes it as hope. The first thing
the Halqa teaches him is how to get *higher*, and he does not notice that it
only ever gets him onto a ledge in a room he is already in.

**Two.** East along the water: the dawn harbour, the salt towers, the neon
crescent. Every sealed way wants a talent and every talent sits behind a
fight, so the Halqa hands him a ladder and charges a fight per rung —
**VAULT**, then **DASH LEAP**, then **POWER KICK**, then **HAYMAKER**. Each
one opens the gate that gives him the next. He thinks he is working his way
out. He is being fitted.

On the neon crescent he meets **AL-SAQR**, who fell too, a long time ago, and
stopped asking. He tells Ahmed so in one line, and Ahmed does not hear it,
because Al-Saqr is a man in a neon strip he is about to beat.

**Three.** Stone island, dead road, the camp of fires where they decide who
fights for the belt. The last wall gives him **HAWK FIST** — fire in his
hands, which is not a thing he brought and not a thing his own world could
have given him. It is the Halqa putting something *into* him, and the moment
he has it he is no longer the man who fell.

**And then the ring closes.** With the camp cleared he can walk west out of
the desert, and west out of the desert is the souq. He has gone all the way
around. He is standing exactly where he landed, with fire in his hands, and
the only door he has never opened is right there — and it is the one his new
hands fit.

**Four.** He opens it. It is not the way up. It is a full house that already
knows his name, and **AL-WAHSH** waiting in the far corner.

He wins. He does not leave.

## The people

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **AHMED / أحمد** | The one who fell. Spelled *Ahmed* in the game's text and code; the author also writes it *Ahmad*. |
| **AL-SAQR / الصقر** — the Falcon | All legs, no patience. Runs the neon crescent. He fell too, years back, and gave up asking about it — which is where the impatience comes from. He is what Ahmed becomes in a year, and he says so, and it does not land. |
| **AL-WAHSH / الوحش** — the Beast | Champion of the Halqa, and the one who has been down here longest. He is not a monster; he is what staying looks like when you are very good at it. Beating him is not defeating him. It is replacing him. |

The rank ladder — ROOKIE, AMATEUR, PROSPECT, RANKED, CONTENDER, CHAMPION — is
the Halqa's own, and it is what the place offers Ahmed *instead* of a door.
That is why it works on him.

## What that means for the game

The three progression systems in all three builds are not mechanics that
happen to be there — they are the plot:

| System | What it is in the story |
| --- | --- |
| **Talents** — found, never bought | What the Halqa teaches him, one fight per rung. Each opens the gate that yields the next, and the last one opens the arena. He reads them as a way out. They are a fitting. |
| **Levels** — earned by fighting | The Halqa's own ladder, and what it offers instead of a door. Every rank is the place telling him he is somebody here. |
| **Upgrades** — bought with XP | Him choosing what kind of fighter he becomes *here*. He does not have to rebuild the man he was, and by the end he has not. |

**HAWK FIST is the turn.** Four talents are ways to cross broken ground —
climb it, leap it, kick it in, punch through it. The fifth is fire in a man's
hands, it comes from the Halqa and nowhere else, and it is the key to the
arena rather than to any gate inside a stage. The moment the player earns it,
Ahmed has taken something from the place that he cannot give back. Nothing
should ever make HAWK FIST feel like the other four.

**The map is the argument, so do not open it.** The loop is closed on purpose,
the arena is at the hub on purpose, and the only non-street link in the world
is the one that leads inward. A shortcut out, a route home, or a tenth area
outside the ring would take the story apart. If the world ever needs to be
bigger, it gets bigger *inside* the wheel.

**Survival mode is the thesis with the story removed** — "no belt, no crowd,
no end, just the next wave." It is what the Halqa is when nobody is dressing
it up.

## What this does not cover yet

Written down honestly rather than smoothed over:

- **The game's text now says the Halqa.** On 2026-09-09 (Riyadh) the nine
  areas were renamed to the places below and every line a player reads about
  them was rewritten for this story: the stage names, hints, story lines and
  their Arabic, the map labels, the victory screen (he wins, he does not
  leave) and the two achievements that named Kuwait. Three things were kept
  on purpose: the product title *AHMED — Kuwait Fighter* and the Almuhallab
  credit, which are the game's name and its maker rather than its world; the
  theme ids in the code (`souq`, `gym`, `fishmarket`, `towers`, `marina`,
  `failaka`, `highway`, `desert`, `arena`), which are internal keys and not
  text; and the press kit under `press/`, which is marketing and still tells
  the old story. The backdrops still draw the shapes they always drew — the
  towers, the dhow, the harbour — so the salt towers *look* like a real
  skyline; that is art, not text, and was not asked for.
- **What the Halqa is** — whether it made itself, whether anyone runs it,
  whether the hole was an accident — is not answered, and the game is stronger
  if it stays that way. Al-Saqr and Al-Wahsh both fell in; neither of them
  knows either.
- **How many others fell.** Every enemy archetype could be a faller, or none
  of them could. Not decided.
